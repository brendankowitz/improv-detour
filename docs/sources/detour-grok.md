# Detour

**A family improv game for the car.** Yes, and the long way.

One phone is the deck. A passenger navigates. The driver never reads the screen. The car earns miles together. There is no loser, no elimination, and no account.

The same 120 cards are the phone game and the print masters for a physical deck.

## Who it is for

- Families in a car, or anyone seated in a circle who cannot stand up and do stage warm-ups.
- About age six and up on Gentle. Family is the default. Bold is stranger and still fully G-rated.
- Two or more riders is the real game. One rider can practice offers out loud.
- Up to six riders. Pass order is the order the names were typed.

## The only rule

Say yes to the last offer, then add one new thing. That is yes-and. You do not have to start with those words unless the How card says so. You do have to treat what they just made true as true.

A star at the end is applause for the offer everyone could use. It is not a point that knocks someone out.

## One round

1. **Deal.** Where, Who, and usually How. A Wild shows up on about one hand in three when Wilds are on.
2. **Open.** The named rider says the first line in character and puts everyone in the place. Do not announce "we are at." Just be there.
3. **Pass.** Clockwise. Each line accepts and adds. The How applies to everybody. The phone shows the next name and, when the How has one, a cue such as "Yes, and…" or "Fortunately…".
4. **Button.** When the timer ends, or sooner via Land it, the current rider says one last line that lands the scene, then stops. That clean ending is the button.
5. **Mile.** Bank it. Optionally tap who made the best yes, and write one journal line.

If a card is not landing, swap that suit before the scene starts, or scrap the detour. A scrap earns no mile.

After Start, the screen is mostly a timer and a name, so the phone can live in a cupholder. The navigator already read the cards. Space bar starts, passes, or lands on a keyboard.

## Suits

Every card has a code, a title, a prompt, a one-line tip, and the skill it trains. Spice is the minimum level: Gentle trips only draw Gentle cards. Family draws Gentle and Family. Bold draws the full deck.

| Suit | Count | Codes | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where | 34 | W-01–W-34 | A place you can stand in. Ordinary-strange, so a kid can picture it without a plot briefing. |
| Who | 33 | C-01–C-33 | The starter's character. Everyone else joins the world and names themselves in their first line. |
| How | 32 | H-01–H-32 | The constraint. Classic workshop games, seated. |
| Wild | 12 | X-01–X-12 | A mile marker. Twists the hand. |
| Pit stop | 9 | P-01–P-09 | A short warm-up between scenes. Finishing one earns a mile. |

### How the How cards teach

- **Accept and build.** Yes-and. The gift. Repeat and raise. Only good news.
- **Specificity.** Name a color. Give it a name. Time words.
- **Listening.** Last word first. One sentence. Questions only.
- **Emotion, played committed.** Delighted, sleepy, proud, polite fury. Serious animal sound. Two-note hum.
- **Genre.** Fairy tale, sports broadcast, cooking show, nature documentary, noir.
- **Safe conflict.** Blame the weather, the traffic, or the soup. Never each other.
- **Car-safe hitchhiker.** Catch the voice: borrow speed, volume, or a pet word. Nobody has to stand up.
- **Risk.** New choice, once. Three words. No "I." An unnamed want.

Some How cards drive the on-screen cue for each line: yes-and, a question, fortunately/unfortunately, the next letter, exactly three words, a gift, or something you can see.

### Wild rules the phone enforces

Most Wilds are read aloud and played socially. Four of them also change the round:

| Card | Effect |
|---|---|
| Four lines | The fourth line is the button. |
| Everybody speaks | Two miles if every rider speaks before the button. Otherwise one. |
| Play it straight | The How card is dropped. |
| Pass the Who | The rider after the usual starter opens. |

The others (window, glovebox object, license plate, same hat, toast, one freeze, mime first, ten-second pile) are instructions. If no license plate is in sight, use the letters in DETOUR.

### Pit stops

The phone keeps the sentence, the letter, or the question so the navigator is not also the scorekeeper. Park it once the car has gone around, and the trip earns one mile.

| Card | What the phone does |
|---|---|
| Yes-and relay | A seed sentence. Each rider starts with "Yes, and…" |
| Fortunately | Alternates Fortunately and Unfortunately from a seed. |
| One-word story | Collects one word at a time and shows the sentence. |
| Window four | Four beats: spot something real, trouble, a plan, a button. |
| Emotional forecast | The same ordinary line, a new feeling each rider. |
| Sell the glovebox | A ridiculous product. About thirty seconds. |
| Alphabet exit | The next letter, A onward. Stop when it stops being fun. |
| The expert | A ridiculous field. Interviewer, then expert. |
| Questions only | A tiny scene. The table marks a real question or a retry. |

### Starting a trip

- Trip name. Default is "Sunday drive."
- One to six rider names.
- Spice: Gentle, Family, or Bold.
- Round length: Short (45s), Classic (75s), Scenic (120s).
- How cards on or off. Off is a plain Where-and-Who scene.
- Wild mile markers on or off.

The first hand is dealt immediately. The opener rotates after each banked scene or pit stop. Riders can be renamed mid-trip. Sound is a short chime on pass, land, and mile, and can be muted.

### What a trip remembers

On this phone only. No sign-in.

- The riders, spice, length, and whether How and Wild are in the deal.
- Miles, and a log of each detour or pit stop: title, the other cards, optional note (about one line), optional applause.
- Which cards have already been seen, so a suit is not repeated until that pile is used up.
- Up to twenty trips. Forgetting a trip removes it. There is no delete-all.

## Paper deck

Print from Deck. The browser print layout is nine cards on a US Letter page, about 2.48 by 3.46 inches, three across and three down. Trim to 2.5 by 3.5 inches and they fit standard poker sleeves.

Printing the full set also lays out a page of card backs. Print faces, flip the paper, print backs. Home printers are fussy. The cut is the craft. A single suit can be printed on its own.

At the table, with no phone:

- Three piles: Where, Who, How. Draw one of each.
- Wilds are a small extra pile, cut in when the drive gets restless.
- Pit stops are their own stack.
- Four lines around the circle is a complete scene. The last line is still the button.
- A finished scene is one mile for the group. Everybody-speaks is two if everyone actually spoke.

## What this is not

- Not a scored competition, a quiz, or a chatbot that writes the scene for you.
- Not multiplayer across phones. One device, passed or read aloud.
- Not a place for romance, scare scenes, or humiliation. Conflict stays sideways: the soup, the weather, the map.
- The driver does not play navigator.

## Design notes

Night-ink screen, paper cards, mile-marker amber, pine for Where, clay for How. The wordmark is a shield. The voice of the copy is a short rulebook: specific, warm, and not cute.
